2.0 The Concept

THE CORE HOOK

Tool-Based Infiltration: Kaito Steal subverts traditional stealth mechanics. Instead of moving a vulnerable character through a hostile environment, players are stationary and must operate a highly flexible, extendable arm to steal artifacts. The design challenge was to shift the player’s mindset from direct spatial navigation to indirect, tool-based trajectory planning.

UNIQUE SELLING POINTS

Unique 'Arm Dragging' control scheme replicable in browser; stealth-puzzle hybrid with very short, high-impact levels (2–4 min each).

3.0 Game Design Document (GDD)

3.1 Core Loop & System Design

TRAJECTORY & TIMING

The core loop is built around precision and prediction. Players must calculate distance, pathing, and extension speed. The arm acts as a physical entity in the world; if any part of it intersects with an enemy's line of sight, the alarm triggers. This transforms the stealth genre into a geometric puzzle where positioning is just as critical as timing.

3.2 Level Design Flow & Pacing

ESCALATING COMPLEXITY (20+ LEVELS)

The game's progression is structured to teach without tutorials. Early levels introduce the arm's basic bending constraints. Mid-levels introduce static hazards and narrow corridors, forcing players to master spatial geometry. Late-game levels introduce dynamic traps (moving lasers, rotating saws) combined with patrolling guards, escalating the mechanical requirement from slow planning to reflex-based execution.

5.0 Key Learnings & Post-Mortem

FRICTIONLESS FAILURE & SKILL EXPRESSION

The most critical decision was regarding the **"Fail State."** Because manipulating the arm requires high precision, failure is frequent. To prevent player churn, I implemented a frictionless, instant-reload upon detection. By removing the punishment of load times, the game encourages a "Trial and Error" loop, allowing players to experiment with bold, creative trajectories without fear.
