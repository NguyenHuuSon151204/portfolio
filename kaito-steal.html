2.0 The Concept

INITIAL IDEA

The concept emerged from a Game Jam prompt: "Control something you can stretch." Instead of a character moving through space, the player controls an extending arm — shifting the spatial interaction model entirely to a tool-based paradigm.

NARRATIVE CONTEXT

Kaito is a master thief who lost his body but kept his arm. Players must navigate museum exhibits, steal artifacts, and avoid robotic guards — all without ever stepping foot in the room.

Concept Sketch — Arm Mechanic Ideation

UNIQUE SELLING POINTS

Unique 'Arm Dragging' control scheme replicable in browser; stealth-puzzle hybrid with very short, high-impact levels (2–4 min each).

3.0 Game Design Document (GDD)

3.1 Core Loop & System Design

CORE LOOP

Observe → Drag arm → Thread gaps → Grab artifact → Retract to safety.
The arm is controlled via click-and-drag. Its trajectory is governed by IK (Inverse Kinematics) — it follows joints around corners. Guards patrol with a fixed Cone of Vision. Players must time their reach to the 1-2s blind spots in patrol cycles.

KEY MECHANIC

Stretching hand physics with joint-based IK. Time-windowed stealth: the arm must be fully retracted before the guard turns back.

FAIL STATE

Hard-fail with instant reload. If the arm is spotted, the level resets immediately — no death animation, no penalty timer. This encourages fast iteration and skill expression over frustration.

3.2 Level Design Flow & Pacing

PROGRESSION STRUCTURE

4 levels, each introducing one new mechanic: Level 1 (drag basics) → Level 2 (corner threading) → Level 3 (moving guards) → Level 4 (dual guards + timed traps).

SAFE ZONE TEACHING

The first half of each level is a "safe practice zone" with no guards. This lets players discover the new mechanic before being tested on it.

Level 4 Layout Sketch

3.3 UI/UX Breakdown

UI is minimalist by design — a heist aesthetic with dark overlays and neon highlights. The focus is on the arm itself as the primary UI element: its color changes (white → red) signals detection state. No separate health bar needed.

UI Flow Wireframe

3.4 Economy & Balancing

No economy system. Balancing is focused entirely on timing windows: guard patrol speed, arm drag speed, and IK response time must form a triangle where the optimal window is 1.5–2.5s — tight enough to create tension but learnable in 2–3 attempts.

4.0 Technical Design Pipeline

IK OPTIMIZATION

Unity's built-in IK was replaced with a custom 2-joint chain solver to reduce WebGL CPU overhead from ~8ms to ~2ms per frame. This was critical for smooth 60fps browser play.

ASSET OPTIMIZATION

All sprites compressed to 512×512 max with Crunch compression. WebGL build size reduced from 48MB to 22MB. Audio compressed to OGG at 96kbps.

5.0 Key Learnings & Post-Mortem

WHAT DIDN'T WORK

Early IK drag felt "floaty" and unresponsive — playtests showed players losing trust in the control scheme. Adding a subtle arm-snap animation on contact made the mechanic feel deliberate and satisfying.

WHAT WORKED WELL

The hard-fail instant-reload was a game design risk that paid off — playtesters averaged 4 attempts per level and described the satisfaction as "like solving a puzzle." Zero frustration reported in sessions.
